The Leather Types Guide

Leather decides how a bag looks, feels and ages. Here is what each type offers, and how to look after it.

  1. Grades of leather
  2. Finishes compared
  3. Tanning: vegetable or chrome
  4. Choosing for your routine
  5. Caring for each type
  6. Signs of quality leather

01

Grades of leather

Full-grain keeps the hide’s complete outer layer; it is the strongest and develops a rich patina. Top-grain is lightly sanded for a more even surface. Split and bonded leathers come from lower layers or fibres and are far less durable.

03

Tanning: vegetable or chrome

Vegetable-tanned leather is firmer, smells rich and darkens noticeably with use. Chrome-tanned leather is softer, more colourfast and more water resistant from the start. Many bags use a combination.

04

Choosing for your routine

  • Daily commute: pebbled or cross-grain; they shrug off knocks.
  • Office: smooth calf for polish, if you can accept a little patina.
  • Wet climates: cross-grain or treated leathers; avoid suede.
  • Evenings: suede, patent or smooth leather in rich colours.

The Leather Matcher suggests a starting point.

05

Caring for each type

  • Smooth and pebbled: wipe with a dry cloth; condition every few months.
  • Cross-grain: wipe clean; rarely needs conditioning.
  • Suede and nubuck: brush gently; use a protector spray; never condition.
  • Patent: wipe with a soft cloth; store away from other colours.

06

Signs of quality leather

Look for natural variation in the grain, a clean cut edge with dense fibres and a rich smell. Uniform, plasticky surfaces and edges that look papery or layered usually mean lower-grade material.
